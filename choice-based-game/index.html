Hamster Game

You can play the game here!

My role in this project was primarily writing and “coding,” whilst my partner did the storyboarding and visuals. Our inspiration for this game was the Henry Stickmin series, a web game series about a thief trying not to get caught… thieving. The game focuses on relatively slapstick / absurd comedy, where you’re met with a handful of choices at every branch, but only one or two of them progresses the story with you alive. Our goal was to create a game with a similar comedic vibe, but more heavily text-based, and starring a hamster on an escape mission from a first grade classroom.


We didn’t actually refine the game very heavily from our received feedback. On submitting our demo, I thought my writing style was a little too wordy and was getting ready to cut it down, but the demo was actually very well-received, so I decided to just roll with it. I don’t write very often (heck, almost never outside of an educational setting), so this project was a great opportunity to have fun and experiment with things, and I’m happy with how it went.


From a storyline perspective, I wish we had a little more on the air of branching paths. The story is very linear as-is, and I think the classroom-to-classroom format just made branching a path off harder to make interesting, especially if they were to converge to a similar ending. I’m not entirely sure how I would have done that differently, as I think the current structure might make multiple runs-through somewhat monotonous.


My biggest struggle with this project was, as usual, communication. For reference, the first time I reached out to my partner outside of class was to send them the final product so they could attach it to their own portfolio. Fortunately, we split the work into relatively independent jobs, so this wasn’t much of an issue, but I still believe that better communication overall would have made this project go a lot smoother, and I think we would have been able to get much more done.


obligatory nerd warning for the following paragraph...


There were a surprising amount of scrapped ideas for this project, at least on my end. I really wanted to sneak some underlying lore into the game, possibly something to allow the hamster to (canonically) retain its memories of past lives, but I couldn’t find a way to explain this that both fell into the bounds of brevity and satisfied my sci-fi-obsessed mind. The closest I got included the hamster being revealed to be a sort of Laplace’s demon; a theoretical superintelligent being that can perfectly observe the position and energy of every atom in the universe and simulate the future accordingly. This idea alone sparked a hefty amount of curiosity and research on my end, and I spent some of the time I should have spent working on this game instead reading about this and similar theoretical constructs (god, I love autism)... so that’s something. I then piled on the idea of introducing the topic using even more concepts from theoretical / quantum physics (e.g. the hamster getting entangled with a student who nearly stepped on them -> the student finding out about their abilities -> something something happily ever after). The idea was thrown out entirely after I realized that (one) there was no possible way I could cohesively flesh out and explain this concept to my partner, much less the entire intended audience of this game, and (two) this is a game about a hamster escaping a classroom, not quantum physics… so just remember what your beloved hamster could have been.
